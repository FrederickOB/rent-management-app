## 4. Design

### 4.1 Modules

- **`messaging`** (layer 7) grows from the M3 core: channels, conversations, inbound processing, the bot, templates and the window, consent capture, broadcasts, push devices and the live inbox stream.
- **`maintenance`** (layer 6, new) holds only `maintenance_job` in its reported state and `MaintenanceService.report()`. Messaging calls it directly (a downward import), so the bot can give the ticket number in the same reply.
- **`EntitlementsService`** (in `companies`) answers `has(company, feature)` from the interim plan setting (decision 9). The policy layer stays the only place that checks roles.
- The `rent/module-layers` lint map gains `maintenance` and the new imports.

### 4.2 Data model (migrations from 0030)

Every company-scoped table has `company_id`, RLS forced and composite foreign keys, as before.

- **`message`** gains:
    - `direction` (outbound, inbound) and `conversation_id`;
    - `author_type` (staff, bot, system, tenant, contact, unknown) and `author_person_id`;
    - `channel` for inbound messages and replies on a fixed channel;
    - `template_key` and `template_version`;
    - `provider` and `provider_message_id`, unique for inbound, so a repeated webhook makes one row.

    Channels and purposes widen to `whatsapp`, `push` and `in_app`, and to the new purposes (conversation reply, bot reply, broadcast, opt-out confirmation, overdue alert). The table stays append-only.
- **`message_event`** gains `read`, and the WhatsApp pricing category and billable flag.
- **`message_attachment`** (append-only): the stored document, content type, size, the provider's media id and when it was copied.
- **`conversation`**:
    - one per tenant per company, unique, or a triage row keyed by channel and address;
    - the state from architecture section 7, assignee, tags, `first_response_due_at`, last inbound and outbound times per channel;
    - mutable, with audit triggers on every change.
- **`channel_identity`**: a phone or email that staff linked to a tenant from triage, with who linked it and when.
- **`whatsapp_account`** (platform table, not company-scoped): Meta's number id, the display number and name, `shared` or `company`, and the owning company for a company number. Read through a narrow system function.
- **`message_template`** (platform-level, versioned) and **`whatsapp_template_status`** (per number, template and language).
- **`bot_session`**: the conversation's bot state and context, expiring after 30 minutes idle.
- **`reply_address`**: the token hash for each conversation's email reply address.
- **`push_device`**: a person's Expo push token per device, tied to their session and dropped when the session is revoked. People span companies (TL-12), so the send job reads tokens through a narrow function that returns only tokens of a person who is a tenant of that company.
- **`tenant_group`** and its members, for broadcast audiences.
- **`broadcast`** and **`broadcast_recipient`**: the audience, text, template, who sent it, its counts, and one message per recipient.
- **`consent_event`** gains sources: `tenant_reply`, `stop_keyword`, `start_keyword`, `tenant_app` and `email_unsubscribe`.
- **Company settings:** `inboxFirstResponseMinutes` (240); the interim plan field, writable only by a platform admin.

### 4.3 Outbound: channels, order and the window

- **The order** for a business-initiated message: push, WhatsApp, SMS, email. Each channel needs an address and consent. A message already narrowed to some channels keeps that list.
- **WhatsApp decides between free text and a template** in one pure function in `packages/domain/messaging`, from the purpose, the window and the template's status on the sending number:
    - inside the window, free text;
    - outside it, the purpose's approved template;
    - with no approved template, WhatsApp is skipped for that message and the next channel is tried. The reason is recorded.
- **Templates** are rendered from our registry. On the shared number the first parameter is always the landlord's name (OQ-24).
- **Documents** (receipts, statements) go on WhatsApp as a document message from a 5-minute presigned link; by email as an attachment; by push and SMS as the existing secure link.
- **Failures:** a retryable failure is retried as in M3. A number that is not on WhatsApp, or a template refused, counts as a permanent failure for that channel, and the next channel is tried (the SMS fallback in CM-09).
- **Delivery reports** from WhatsApp, Hubtel and Postmark, and Expo push receipts, become `delivered`, `read` or `failed` events.

### 4.4 Inbound

1. **Intake:** the webhook endpoint stores the raw request in `webhook_inbox` (M4b's table), one row per message or status inside a batch, verifies the signature (Meta's `X-Hub-Signature-256`, Hubtel's and Postmark's configured secrets), and returns 2xx.
2. **`messaging.process-inbound`** (idempotent by provider and message id):
    - resolves the company: a company number gives it directly; the shared number uses decision 4;
    - matches the sender (tenant, contact, linked identity, or unknown);
    - records the message and attachments, then updates the conversation.
3. **STOP, STOP ALL, UNSUBSCRIBE and START** are recognised first, whatever state the conversation is in.
    - On the shared number, routing comes before STOP when the phone is known to several companies (founder decision, 2026-11-04).
4. **Then**, on WhatsApp, the bot handles it if the conversation is with the bot; on other channels, or once handed over, it goes to the inbox.
5. **`messaging.fetch-media`** copies each media item to storage at once, checking type and size. Media is served only through short-lived links after a policy check.
6. **Email:** the token in the reply address finds the conversation, and the sender must be the email on record. Postmark's stripped reply is shown; the full message is stored.
7. **In-app:** a tenant writing in the app posts straight to their conversation through the API.

### 4.5 The menu bot (CM-03)

- **A pure state machine** in `packages/domain/messaging/bot.ts`: (state, context, input) to (new state, actions). The actions are replies, a document to send, a pay link, a repair report, or a handover. The messaging service carries the actions out.
- **States:** menu, choose lease (a tenant with several), receipts list, repair (collecting a description and media until DONE or 10 minutes pass), handed over.
- **Input:** WhatsApp reply buttons and list rows, with numbered text ("reply 1 for balance") as a fallback.
- **Who sees money:** the tenant's own phone, and contacts marked as billing or signatory for a company tenant. Other contacts get "talk to a person".
- **Figures** come from the billing reads: amount due now, paid-until, the next due date. The bot never computes a balance itself.
- **Handover:** the conversation opens in the inbox at once and the response timer starts. A tenant who writes again after a resolved conversation starts with the bot again on WhatsApp.

### 4.6 The inbox (CM-05, CM-06)

- **Visible to:** staff whose scope includes any property of the tenant's leases (policy). Triage is visible to agency admins and managers.
- **Thread:** every message on every channel in order, with delivery status per message, attachments, and the window's remaining time.
- **Replying:** the composer defaults to the channel the tenant last used. On WhatsApp outside the window it offers only the "reopen conversation" template, or a switch to SMS.
- **Assigning and tagging:** assign to a staff member in scope; tags from a company list. Both are audited.
- **States:** resolve, and reopen on the tenant's next message, as in architecture section 7.
- **Overdue:** `messaging.sla-check` (every 5 minutes, idempotent per conversation and deadline) alerts the assignee, or the managers, by email and, for staff who opted in, WhatsApp (OQ-33).
- **Live updates:**
    - a trigger on message and conversation changes sends a `NOTIFY` with ids only;
    - the API holds one `LISTEN` connection and pushes server-sent events to each signed-in client, after a policy check per conversation;
    - the client then fetches the changed thread.

### 4.7 Broadcasts (CM-08)

- **Audience:** a property, blocks or a tenant group, limited to current tenants in the sender's scope.
- **Preview:** how many can be reached, on which channel, and who cannot (no consent, no address).
- **Sending:** on confirmation, `messaging.broadcast-fanout` queues one message per recipient in batches of 50, throttled per provider. WhatsApp outside the window uses an approved building-notice template.
- **Stats:** queued, sent, delivered, read, failed and not sent, per channel.
- **Who may:** staff with `messaging.broadcast` (agency admins, managers and landlords). No approval step.

### 4.8 Consent (CM-09)

- **Recorded by staff** as in M3, now for WhatsApp and push as well.
- **WhatsApp reply:** a known tenant writing in on WhatsApp without consent there is asked once whether they want reminders and receipts on WhatsApp. YES records it.
- **App toggle:** recorded with the app as source.
- **STOP and START** as decided (decision 7).
- **Email:** an unsubscribe page in `apps/links` (`/u/[token]`) withdraws email.
- **Phone or email changes** as decided (decision 8).
- **Exempt:** the M4b settlement alerts and staff notices, one-time codes, and conversation replies (decision 6).

### 4.9 Wiring existing messages

Receipts (BC-06), rent reminders (BC-09), renewal reminders (TL-09), paid-until prompts (AR-06), signing links and reminders, and ID-check links widen their channel lists to push and WhatsApp, each with its template. Push opens the matching screen in the app. Nothing about when or why they go changes.

Review-round deviations, each the easier to reverse:

1. **A keyword on the shared number goes to one landlord** (founder decision, 2026-11-04, replacing the reversible default the review proposed). "Ask 'Which landlord?' first. Until they answer, every landlord can still send to them."
    - On the shared WhatsApp number or by SMS, routing comes first, in decision 4's order: the company that last messaged the phone on that number within 24 hours, then the only company that knows the phone, then the question.
    - STOP, STOP ALL or START then applies in the routed or chosen company only. Under the first two rules that happens at once.
    - A keyword waiting on the question applies once the tenant answers, when the waiting messages are processed into the chosen company.

2. **STOP typed in the app stops SMS** (decision 1: until there is a short code, the app is how a tenant stops SMS). STOP ALL stops every channel, and START gives SMS back. 3. **The window starts at the pipeline's time, dated back only for a delay over a minute** (S4). Provider timestamps are to the second; our consent and conversation records order to the millisecond.
4. **A keyword's consent event takes the keyword's time, but never earlier than an event already on record for that channel.** So the newest stays newest (S3).
5. **A message is filed under the property of the tenant's latest issued lease when they have no current one** (MF-2). Messages still carry one property, so a caretaker of a two-lease tenant's other property does not see them (not changed).
6. **Broadcast previews last 30 minutes** (a product choice). The token carries its issue time; within that time the same confirmation is one broadcast.
7. **An unsubscribe link bound to its address works past its date** (opt-outs are always honoured). Links issued before the binding still expire.
8. **Unknown senders may have 3 media items per message and 10 a day copied** (a product choice, S11). A webhook source sending more than 20 bad requests in 15 minutes is refused without being stored (S-2).
9. **Any late delivery failure falls back to the next channel**, whatever its code (S6). A failed push is marked failed, but its dead device token is only forgotten at the next send (as before).
10. **The pay link's availability counts push and WhatsApp consent as well.** It does not check the WhatsApp window and template, which billing cannot read. The send itself uses the exact planned channels.
11. **Signing messages to a tenant or their contact may go by WhatsApp and push** (S7). WhatsApp is used only when the snapshotted signer phone is still the one on record. These emails now carry a reply address and, when business-initiated, the unsubscribe link.
12. **The seed keeps Kofi Arrears-45 and Ama Tenant SMS-only** (E2). No M6 spec relied on their WhatsApp consent.

## 8. Risks

1. **Meta verification and template approval** take weeks (OQ-24). Everything runs on the fake until then; a template that is not approved falls back to SMS.
2. **The shared number speaks for many landlords.** Meta's policy bars messaging "in the voice of another business" without permission (OQ-40, counsel). Landlord terms must grant it before the pilot.
3. **SMS replies need a short code** (decision 1). Until then, a tenant without WhatsApp or the app cannot answer by SMS.
4. **Live updates in 10 seconds** rely on one `LISTEN` connection per API instance. If the deployment adds instances, each needs its own connection.
5. **Raw webhook bodies hold message text and phone numbers.** They stay in the database, never in logs. Their retention joins OQ-14.
6. **Ghana's SMS send window:** Arkesel's guide mentions a promotional window of 8 a.m. to 7 p.m. with no Sundays. This milestone sends no promotional SMS, but I will add the question to `docs/open-questions.md` for counsel rather than assume the rule.

### Deviations that change behaviour

Every deviation is in sections 5.1 to 5.3. The ones that change behaviour:
1. **"Which landlord?" is not recorded as a message,** so nothing reaches a landlord before the tenant chooses (TL-12).
2. **Overdue alerts go by email only,** because staff WhatsApp needs an opt-in (OQ-33).
3. **TL-09 renewal reminders stay staff-only.**
4. **STOP typed in the app stops SMS.** Until there is a short code, the app is how a tenant stops SMS. STOP ALL stops every channel.
5. **A tenant without a current lease** has their messages filed under the property of their latest issued lease.
6. **Unsubscribe links:** bound to their address, they work past their date.
7. **Unknown senders:** at most 3 media items per message and 10 a day.
8. **Pay links:** availability counts push and WhatsApp consent.
9. **Push:** without an EAS project id, the app offers no notifications.
10. **Photos:** attaching photos works only in the tenant app's web build until `expo-image-picker` is added.
