## 4. Design

### 4.2 Notice types and their fields

| Type | Mandatory fields | Period | Ladder default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rent demand and arrears | Arrears as of a date (from the ledger), invoices covered, pay-by date | none; link 90 days | full |
| Rent review or increase | Current rent, new rent, effective date, reason | contractual | full |
| Notice to quit | Ground (arrears, breach, end of term, landlord's own use, other), particulars, effective date | contractual, or statutory once verified | full, plus posting on the premises (decision 10) |
| Notice of entry | Date and time window, purpose (inspection, repairs) | none recorded; warns | reminders only |
| Breach of tenancy | Kind (noise, unauthorised occupants, sub-letting, damage, other), particulars, remedy-by date | none recorded; warns | full |

| Building-wide (fire safety, major works) | Property or blocks, title, details, dates | none; link 90 days | reminders only |
| Tenant-originated (intention to vacate, complaint) | Date received, how, document | recorded only (decision 13) | none |

A building-wide notice has one recipient per current tenant of the property or blocks chosen, each with their own link and record.

### 4.3 The earliest lawful effective date

A pure function in `packages/domain/notices`, using `packages/domain/dates` only:

```
service date   = the issue date, when the lease has the electronic-service clause and the notice goes electronically
               = the date physical service is recorded, otherwise
period (days)  = the larger of:
                   the lease's notice days, for types that use the contractual period
                   the statutory minimum from the rules table, when its value is not null
earliest date  = service date + period days
non-renewal    = the later of the lease end date and the earliest date
```

- **Unverified rules.** Every rule the calculation reads is recorded with the notice by rule version. While a rule it depends on is null or `unverified`, the composer shows a prominent warning naming it.
- **Blocking.** The composer refuses an effective date earlier than the earliest date. The check runs again when the notice is issued, because approval may take days; if the date no longer holds, issue is refused and the notice goes back to draft with the reason.
- **Late physical service.** Without the clause, the composer calculates from the planned issue date and warns that the period runs from physical service. When service is recorded, the tracker checks again. If the period from the service date ends after the stated effective date, the notice is flagged "served too late for its date" and offers a correction (4.8).
- **The s.23 bar** (decision 3) blocks a notice to quit inside 24 months of a recorded assessment.

### 4.4 The notice state machine

Architecture section 7 holds the diagram. In summary:

- Draft → PendingApproval (submit) → Approved → Sent (issued).
- Sent → Delivered → Opened → Signed, with Reminded steps between.
- Reminded → Escalated (day 7, service pack) → Served (physical service recorded).
- Signed or Served → Closed (outcome).
- Draft → Cancelled (discarded).

Rules:

- **The status is the furthest point reached;** every step is also a row in `notice_event` with its server timestamp. A late signature after escalation is still recorded as Signed.
- **Pausing or cancelling the ladder** is a flag on the notice, not a state, with who, when and why.
- **For commercial tenants** each recipient has its own events, and the notice is Signed when any authorised signatory signs.
- **The transitions** live in `packages/domain/notices`, so the service cannot skip a state.

### 4.5 Signing on the M3 engine

- **The subject:** `signing_request.subject_type` gains `notice`.
- **Completion:** a new completion rule, `any_one`, beside M3's `all_required`, for LN-07.
- **No decline button:** a tenant cannot decline receipt, and an unsigned notice is never blocked (`CLAUDE.md` section 9).
- **Consent:** a separate consent text, `notice_ack.v1`, says that signing acknowledges receipt, not agreement. The page says the same above the signature box.
- **Links** follow decision 6. Each records its first open: server time, IP and user agent.
- **Evidence:** the frozen document, the one-time code, the signature evidence and the certificate page are M3's. The certificate page shows the verified phone, the device details, the server time in UTC and in the company's time zone, and both hashes. Both hashes go into the audit chain.
- **The tenant app** lists notices under "To sign" (its wording made general) and opens the `apps/links` page.

### 4.6 Issue and delivery

1. **Approval** records who approved: one person, or two for a notice to quit.
2. **`NoticesService.issue`** runs in one transaction:
    - checks the effective date and the bar again;
    - takes the next notice number;
    - snapshots the fields;
    - requests the frozen PDF.
3. **When the PDF is recorded** with its SHA-256, the signing request opens. One message per recipient per channel is queued at the same moment, with M6's `deliver to all` mode:
    - WhatsApp, with the notice-link template;
    - SMS;
    - email, with the PDF attached and the link.

    Channels follow decisions 4 and 5. A channel that cannot be used is recorded on the notice with its reason.
4. **Delivery reports** from M6 become `delivered` events with the confirming channel (prd-issues 7). Email counts as delivered only when the provider reports delivery.

### 4.7 The ladder and escalation

- **`notices.ladder`** runs every 15 minutes, per company. For each unsigned recipient of a live notice, it takes the steps that are due, from the type's settings, idempotent per recipient and step:
    - **24 hours:** a reminder by WhatsApp and SMS;
    - **72 hours:** a reminder by WhatsApp, SMS and email, stating that the notice remains in force whether or not it is signed;
    - **5 days:** the manager is alerted, and a task asks the caretaker to remind the tenant in person;
    - **7 days:** escalation.
- **Reminders** carry a fresh link (decision 6) and wait for quiet hours (decision 11).
- **The ladder stops** when the recipient signs, or for every recipient when a commercial signatory signs. It also stops when the notice closes, or when staff pause or cancel it.
- **`notices.escalate`** builds the physical service pack: a cover sheet, the frozen notice, and a service affidavit template with the notice's details filled in. The affidavit's wording is counsel's (OQ-10), shown as a placeholder until then. The notice becomes Escalated, and a physical-service task is created.
- **Recording service:** method (handed to the tenant, posted on the premises, left with an adult occupant, registered post, courier), the date and time, who served it, a witness, a photo of the posted notice, and notes. A record is never edited; a correction is a new record that references it.

### 4.8 Immutability, corrections and closure

- **Once issued,** a database trigger refuses changes to a notice's fields, dates, recipients, document and hashes. Only its status, ladder flags and outcome move, and each move is audited.
- **Correcting** an issued notice starts a new draft that references it. The new notice says which notice it replaces. When the new notice is issued, the old one closes as `superseded` and its ladder stops. Its links still open the old document, with a banner naming the new notice.
- **Closing** needs `notices.close`, an outcome and a note. Closing a notice to quit offers only the outcomes in decision 12; nothing ends the lease or touches the unit. Move-out stays in M8.
- **Every status change** writes a `notice_event`, appears on the tenant's timeline, and is in the audit log.

### 4.9 Data model (migrations after M6's)

- **`notice`:**
    - number, type and subtype, lease, tenant, property and unit;
    - the frozen field snapshot, issue date, effective date and earliest lawful date;
    - the rule versions used, and whether the clause applied;
    - the corrected notice, the document and its SHA-256, and the approval request;
    - the ladder flags, outcome, note, and who closed it and when.
- **`notice_recipient`:** the recipient's name, phone and email as snapshotted, their signer, and the channels withheld with reasons.
- **`notice_event`** (append-only): the notice, the recipient where it applies, the kind, the channel, the server time, the actor and details.
- **`service_record`** (append-only): as in 4.7, with the photo and an uploaded sworn affidavit as documents.
- **`notice_counter`:** per company, gap-free.
- **`rent_assessment`** (append-only): the unit, the date, the reference and the document.
- **`notice_quit_acknowledgement`** (append-only): grant or withdraw, by whom, the statement version and its hash.
- **`task`:**
    - kind, title, property, related record, assignee, due time;
    - status (open, done, cancelled), who completed it and the note;
    - mutable, with audit triggers.
- **Company settings:** the ladder per notice type, quiet hours (07:00 to 20:00), and the link expiry for types without a period (90 days).
- **Rules:** `notice_to_quit.assessment_bar_months` (24, `unverified`). The statutory minimum keys already exist with null values.

## 8. Risks

1. **Notice periods and electronic service are unverified** (OQ-09, OQ-10). Notices to quit stay behind the acknowledgement, two-person approval and the composer's warnings until counsel answers.
2. **The legal wording is placeholder text** (decision 1). Notices should not go to real tenants until counsel supplies it.
3. **Delivered depends on provider reports** (prd-issues 7). Email is the weakest; the tracker says which channel confirmed delivery.
4. **Late physical service** can make a stated date too early (4.3). The product flags it and offers a correction; it cannot prevent it.
5. **The ladder is timing-sensitive.** Tests run on a fixed clock through `packages/domain/dates`, never the wall clock.

### Deviations that change behaviour

Every deviation is in section 9. The ones that change behaviour:
1. **Co-tenants acknowledge by link only.** They are lease parties without a tenant record, so the tenant app does not list their notice.
2. **A rent demand is checked against the ledger as it stands at submit and issue,** not as of its "as of" date.
3. **An approval that closes without approving** sends the notice back to draft on its next write or on the ladder sweep.
4. **`tasks.manage`** (new) lets managers cancel tasks.
5. **"Served in person"** (handed over, or left with an adult) skips escalation. Posting on the premises still escalates.
6. **The seed renders PDFs with Playwright's Chromium,** so CI installs Chromium before seeding, and a new machine needs `pnpm exec playwright install chromium` once.
