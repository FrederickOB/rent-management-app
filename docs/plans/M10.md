## 4. Design

### 4.1 Modules

- **`subscription`** (layer 8, new): plans, the price list, snapshots, invoices, payments, receipts, entitlements, dunning, tier changes and cancellation.
- **`admin`** (layer 8, new): onboarding, support access, usage, system health and rules.
- **`EntitlementsService`** reads `company.plan` instead of `interim_plan`. The two places that read the plan directly (`messaging/operations.service.ts`, `companies.service.ts`) go through it.
- **P26 posting functions** live in `packages/domain/src/ledger/subscription-posting.ts` and go through `PostingService` in the platform company's context.
- **Pure rules** live in `packages/domain/src/subscription/` (no I/O), each property-tested:
    - billable units per month (reusing `billableInMonth`, D8);
    - the bill, with the minimum and proration by days;
    - discounts and the annual draw;
    - VAT lines;
    - the state machine;
    - the read-only allowlist.

### 4.2 The platform book and P26

- **The platform book:** `app.create_company_book()` also makes a book for a platform company, and a migration adds one for an existing platform company. It gets a unique index of its own.
- **P26 entries:**

  | Event | Debit | Credit | Key |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Invoice | 1100 | 4600, plus VAT and levies payable when charged | `P26:<invoice>` |
  | Payment | 1050 | 1100, or 2020 when it is annual prepayment | `P26:<payment>` |
  | Monthly draw on prepaid credit | 2020 | 1100 | `P26:<invoice>:prepaid` |
  | Settlement of the platform's Paystack balance | 1010 and 5200 | 1050 | `P26:<settlement>` |

  A payment reversal is built from the payment's allocations, as P21 is.
- **No rent entry ever lands in the platform book, and no subscription entry lands in an owner or company book.** A test asserts both directions.

### 4.3 The snapshot and the invoice

- **`subscription.snapshot`** (1st, 00:30, per subscriber company, in the subscriber's own context) lists every unit with a lease that was active on any day of last month (D8), with:
    - the unit code;
    - the band as at the snapshot;
    - the lease;
    - the days it was leased.

  It writes the snapshot through a narrow function into the platform company's tables. The snapshot holds counts by band and the unit lines, frozen.
- **`subscription.invoice`** (1st, 01:00) prices the snapshot with the price list in force for the month and the tier days from the subscription's history. It adds:
    - the minimum top-up;
    - the discount line;
    - VAT lines when charged.

  It numbers the invoice gap-free (`SUB-2026-000001`), posts P26, draws on prepaid credit, renders the PDF, and tells the company's admins.
- **Rounding** happens only in `packages/domain/money`, once per line.

### 4.4 Paying

- **Adapter:** the Paystack adapter gains `initializePlatform` and `chargePlatformMomo` (no subaccount). The fake provider accepts them.
- **Webhooks:** intake is unchanged (inbox, signature check, then a job). The job sends `SUB-` references to the subscription service, which:
    1. verifies the payment with the provider;
    2. compares amount, currency, status and reference;
    3. posts P26;
    4. allocates the payment to the oldest open invoice, or to prepaid credit;
    5. issues a gap-free receipt (`SUBR-2026-000001`);
    6. restores full access if nothing is past due.
- **Reconciliation:** the daily run recognises platform transactions and settlements. A settlement of the platform's balance posts P26's settlement entry.

### 4.5 Status, read-only and dunning

- **`subscription.status` changes only through the state machine** (decisions 5, 11 and 12):
    - Trialing;
    - Active;
    - PastDue;
    - ReadOnly;
    - CancelScheduled;
    - Cancelled.

  Each transition is an append-only `subscription_event`, audited in both companies' chains.
- **`company.access`** is set by the engine through a narrow function.
- **The policy check:** `PolicyService.can` refuses actions outside the read-only allowlist when the actor's company is `read_only`. The allowlist is in the domain and covered by a test over every action. Tenant actors and system jobs are never affected.
- **`subscription.dunning`** (daily, 09:00) sends the reminders and applies read-only. **`subscription.lifecycle`** (daily, 00:15) ends trials, applies downgrades and cancellations on the 1st, and closes the annual terms.

### 4.6 Support access

- **`support_grant`** (customer company, append-only, plus a revocation): who granted it, the reason, the period, revoked at and by whom.
- **`app.current_company_id()`:** for `actor_kind = 'platform_admin'` and any company but the platform's, it returns NULL unless `app.support_grant_active(company, user)` is true. Every RLS policy already uses it.
- **The session:** `withSupport(companyId)` opens a read-only transaction and appends `support.request` to the customer's chain (route, record) in a separate transaction first.
- **Policy:** a platform admin under a grant gets read actions only.

### 4.7 Data model (migrations from 0057)

- **Platform company tables** (`company_id` = the platform company, RLS on it, composite keys):
    - `subscription`;
    - `subscription_event`;
    - `billing_snapshot` and `billing_snapshot_unit`;
    - `subscription_invoice` and `subscription_invoice_line`;
    - `subscription_payment` and `subscription_allocation`;
    - `subscription_receipt`;
    - `discount_code` and `discount_redemption`.

  Money and evidence are append-only. Invoice and receipt counters are gap-free.
- **Platform tables** (no company, read through narrow functions):
    - `price_list` (tier, band rates, minimum, annual discount, effective dates, placeholder flag);
    - `subscription_terms` (trial days, grace days, the read-only switch, due days, retention after cancellation, the VAT switch);
    - `whatsapp_rate`;
    - `alert_event`.

  Each is versioned or append-only.
- **Customer company tables:** `support_grant`, with RLS and audit.
- **`company`** gains `plan` (starter, growth, portfolio; filled from `interim_plan`, which is then dropped) and `access`.
- **Rules:** a new row, `vat.platform_subscription`, `unverified`.

### 4.8 Screens

## 6. Tests

★ marks the playbook's and the PRD's.

- ★ **A unit with an active lease on any day of the month is always billed** (property-based on D8, with leases starting, ending and holding over across month ends).
- ★ **A vacant unit is never billed,** including reserved (signed, not started), under repair and not lettable.
- ★ **A company in arrears, read-only included, still:**
    - takes a tenant's provider payment (webhook to receipt);
    - records cash with its receipt;
    - serves pay links;
    - runs an issued notice's ladder and signing;
    - sends rent reminders.
- ★ **Support access without consent is refused:** by the API, and by the database when the code is bypassed. With a grant it is read-only and audited, and it ends at expiry or revocation.
- **The bill:**
    - never below the minimum;
    - proration by days on an upgrade and on the trial's end;
    - the downgrade next cycle;
    - discounts;
    - the annual draw and its shortfall;
    - VAT lines off by default and correct when on;
    - each one rounded once.
- **Read-only:** a test over every policy action against the allowlist, and paying restores access at once.
- **Money:**
    - P26 entries balance in the platform book;
    - no rent in the platform book and no subscription money in an owner book;
    - a webhook replay is idempotent;
    - invoice and receipt numbers are gap-free under concurrency;
    - a payment whose verified amount differs is refused.
- **Isolation:**
    - cross-company isolation for every new table;
    - a subscriber never sees another subscriber's invoices or snapshots;
    - log redaction covers the new payloads.
- **Entitlements:** every plan feature on every tier, and a locked feature names its tier.
- **End to end (web):**
    - a subscriber pays an invoice by card on the fake provider and gets the receipt;
    - an upgrade shows on the next invoice;
    - the read-only banner shows and lifts on payment;
    - support access asked for, granted and used read-only;
    - a platform admin onboards a company and changes a price for next month.

## 8. Risks

1. **The read-only check must never stop a tenant flow.** It sits in the policy layer, with an allowlist and a test over every action, and tenant actors and system jobs are outside it.
2. **The support guard changes `app.current_company_id()`,** which every RLS policy calls. It adds a lookup only for platform admins, and tests cover performance and every actor kind.
3. **Mixing platform money with rent (D1)** is guarded by separate references (`SUB-`), separate books and tests in both directions.
4. **Changing where the plan comes from** touches about 14 services. They already go through `EntitlementsService`, and the two that don't are fixed first.
5. **Placeholder prices and VAT** could be mistaken for real ones. The console labels placeholders and unverified VAT, and VAT is off by default.

### Backend

Each is easy to reverse unless it says otherwise.

1. **Subscribers read their own rows through a select-only policy, not definer functions (decision 1).**
    - Each platform company table has a second, `FOR SELECT` policy, `subscriber_read`, on `subscriber_company_id = app.current_company_id()`; lines and snapshot units go through their parent.
    - Writes still need the platform company's context, and the restrictive `no_tenant_session` policy keeps every tenant session out.
    - The effect is the same as narrow functions, and the services use typed queries. Tested: a subscriber never sees another's invoices or snapshots, by service and by raw SQL.
    - **Corrected in the review (BE-22):** the select-only policy was not enough on its own. A subscriber's staff session could insert rows of its own, because the isolation policy compares the transaction's company only. 0065 adds restrictive write policies on the 12 tables: only the system or a platform admin writes subscription records. A raw-SQL sweep over every `subscriber_read` table now proves reading and writing.
2. **`interim_plan` is replaced in three steps:** 0057 adds `company.plan` and `company.access`, 0058 copies each company's plan in its own context (audited), and 0059 drops `interim_plan`. `app.company_plans()` now returns `plan`. M6's `companyPlans` and `setCompanyPlan` routes stay, on `plan`; `setCompanyPlan` still takes Starter or Growth only, and `platformAdmin.setTier` is the audited route for any tier.
3. **A platform admin's own context never enters a customer company without a grant (decision 13), so three M6 paths changed how they write there.** M6's WhatsApp-number audit event in the customer's chain, `app.set_company_plan` and `app.create_company` (onboarding) now write as the platform's system path with the admin's person id. Those events record actor kind `system`, not `platform_admin`.
    - **Corrected in the review (BE-16):** the database guard holds only for a context declared `platform_admin`, so the build still let platform-admin requests read and write customers through `withCompany(..., 'system')`. Now no platform-admin request opens a customer company at all:
