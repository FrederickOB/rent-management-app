**No overlapping leases.** An exclusion constraint (`btree_gist`) prevents two leases on one unit from overlapping while either is signed, active, ending or holding over.

### 4.3 People and tenant sign-in (TL-01, OQ-30, `CLAUDE.md` section 5)

- **One tenant record per company.** Each company keeps its own record for an individual tenant. The record is linked by phone number to a global person through `app.ensure_tenant_person(phone)`.
    - This `SECURITY DEFINER` function returns an id only.
    - It never says whether the person already existed, so company A cannot learn that company B has this tenant.
- **Phone changes.** Changing a tenant's phone re-links that company's record to the person for the new number. It never edits the global person.
- **Duplicates.** A phone number or ID number (by blind index) that is already on another tenant in the company gives a warning, with a link to that tenant.
- **Company tenants** have no person of their own. Their signatories sign through links; portal users are P2.
- **The tenant app.**
    1. `app.tenancies_for_person(person_id)` returns company and tenant ids only.
    2. The API then reads each company's data under that company's context, with the scope set to that tenant (`app.tenant_scope`).
    3. Restrictive RLS policies on the tenant tables limit what a tenant session sees.
- **Staff scope** extends to tenancies. A caretaker or agency-client owner sees the tenants and leases on properties in their scope, through the M2 property-scope predicate.

### 4.4 ID numbers and verification (TL-02, `CLAUDE.md` section 10)

- **Ghana Card number.**
    - Checked against the format `GHA-` plus 9 digits, a hyphen and a check digit.
    - Encrypted with M1's field cipher, which binds the ciphertext to its company, table, column and row.
    - Given a blind index for duplicate checks, and masked by default (`GHA-*******89-0`).
- **Verification:**
    1. Staff start a check, which calls `IdentityVerificationPort.startCheck`.
    2. The capture link goes to the tenant by SMS, if consent allows.
    3. `identity.verify-id` polls for the result after 5 minutes, 30 minutes, 2 hours, 12 hours and 24 hours. After that, staff can press "check again".
- **Statuses:** not started, pending, verified, not matched. A provider failure leaves the check pending and it is retried (OQ-22).
- **What is stored:** the check id, status and times only; the provider keeps the capture.
- **Passports:** the number and issuing country, encrypted, with the status "recorded, not verified".
- **Reveal** (`tenant.id.reveal`):
    - **who:** a landlord in their own company, an agency admin or a manager;
    - **what it needs:** a reason, and it writes the audit event `id_number.revealed` (who, when, why and which tenant, never the number);
    - **who never sees the number:** caretakers, accountants, advisers and agency-client owners (PRD section 15).
- **TINs** for owners and company tenants are encrypted and masked the same way, because an individual's TIN is their Ghana Card number.
- **Logs.** The redaction property test gains passport and TIN patterns.

### 4.5 Lease templates (TL-05, decision 3)

- **Structure.** A template is a list of sections, each holding clauses. Each clause has:
    - a key and a title;
    - a body of plain-text paragraphs and numbered lists;
    - a required flag and an editable flag.
- **Placeholders.**
    - Bodies may use placeholders from a fixed list, such as `{{tenant.name}}`, `{{unit.code}}`, `{{lease.startDate}}` and `{{rent.monthly}}`.
    - They are filled from the lease's frozen data.
    - A version that uses an unknown placeholder is refused.
- **Required clauses:** parties, premises, term, rent, electronic service and signatures. These clauses cannot be removed.
- **Versions** are immutable. Publishing creates a new version, and a lease keeps the version it was built from.
- **Rendering.**
    - One lease document template in `packages/documents` goes from React to HTML to PDF on the branded letterhead.
    - The signing page shows the same rendered HTML, stored with its own hash, so signers read exactly what the PDF holds.
- **Counsel review.**
    - Each version carries a "reviewed by counsel" flag, which a company admin sets with a note.
    - Until it is set, the builder and the draft show a warning.
- **Starter templates.**
    - The three templates are seeded into each company on first use.
    - The electronic-service clause names the tenant's phone numbers and email (prd-issues 25).

### 4.6 Lease terms and the rent schedule (TL-06)

- **Terms:**

    | Term | Rule |
    | --- | --- |
    | Start and end dates | Required; M3 leases are fixed term. |
    | Monthly rent | Money, in pesewas. |
    | Billing cycle | Monthly, quarterly, six-monthly or annual. |
    | Initial advance | Optional: a number of months the first invoice covers, such as 24. |
    | Deposit and monthly service charge | Optional. |
    | Escalation steps | Each step is a date and a new monthly rent. |
    | Break clause | From when, who may break, the notice in days, and whether unused days of the current month are credited (prd-issues 36). |
    | Contractual notice | In days. |
    | Use | Residential or commercial, defaulted from the unit's band. |
    | Electronic-service contacts | Feed the electronic-service clause. |

- **Validation** in `packages/domain`:
    - the end is after the start;
    - escalation steps fall inside the term, on the start of a rent period, each later than the last;
    - amounts are positive whole numbers of pesewas;
    - the initial advance is at least one billing cycle and at most the whole term;
    - the cycle is no longer than the term.
- **Schedule** (`charge_period`):
    - **Monthly periods** anchored on the start date, whatever the billing cycle.
    - **Anchoring.** Each period start is computed from the anchor, not from the previous period, so a lease starting on 31 January runs 31 Jan, 28 Feb, 31 Mar.
    - **Short final period.** A short last period is prorated by days and rounded half up once, in `packages/domain/money`.
    - **Rows.** Rent and service charge get separate rows.
    - **When.** The schedule is generated when the lease is signed, and it is append-only.
    - **Holding over** adds one period a month at the last rate (OQ-12).
    - **Invoices** group the periods by the billing cycle; that is M4a.

### 4.7 Lease lifecycle and occupancy (PB-09)

The states follow the architecture's lease machine, plus holding over.

| From | To | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Draft | Pending signatures | Issued: the document is frozen and a signing request opens. |
| Pending signatures | Draft | Recalled (links revoked, request voided), or a signer declined. |
| Pending signatures | Signed | Finalised after the last required signature. |
| Signed | Active | The start date arrives (`tenancy.lease-transitions`, daily at 00:05). |
| Active | Holding over | The end date passes with no renewal starting and no move-out recorded. |
| Active or holding over | Ending | Staff record non-renewal or a notice. |
| Active, holding over or ending | Ended | Staff record a move-out date (the minimal TL-11 hook). |
| Draft or pending signatures | Cancelled | Staff cancel the lease. |
| Signed, before the start date | Cancelled | With two-person approval (decision 6). |

Early termination with refunds arrives with AR-04 (M5) and TL-11 (M8).

**Occupancy:**
- **Status.** A unit is occupied while it has a lease that is active, holding over or ending, and reserved between signature and start; otherwise it is vacant. A view derives this; nothing stores it.
- **History.** A lease status change writes an occupancy row to `unit_status_event` in the same transaction, through a trigger that runs as the table owner.
- **Guard.** A direct insert of an occupancy row by `rent_app` is refused.
- **Availability conflicts.** While a unit is occupied, its availability can only be "available" or "under repair".

### 4.8 E-signature engine (TL-07, `CLAUDE.md` section 9)

The flow follows architecture section 9. The detail:

- **Freezing the document.** When a lease is issued, the API builds a data snapshot: tenant, unit, landlord or owner, terms, parties and clauses. It then:
    1. renders the HTML and stores it in write-once storage with its hash;
    2. asks `documents.render-pdf` for the PDF, which goes to the `evidence` bucket under Object Lock with its SHA-256.

    The signing request opens once the PDF exists.
- **Signers** for a lease:
    - the tenant, or each required signatory of a company tenant;
    - the landlord's signatory, an owner or a manager named on the lease;
    - the guarantor, if there is one;
    - the witness, when the company requires one.

    Each signer's name and phone or email are snapshotted. Codes go to the phone, or to the email for signatories who have only an email (prd-issues 26).
- **One signing page for everyone.** The landlord's signatory signs on the same links page with a code sent to them, like every other signer.
- **Links.**
    - A link is created by the delivery job, so its raw token is never stored. The token is 256 random bits, and only its SHA-256 is kept.
    - Each link serves one signer for the `sign` purpose and expires after 30 days.
    - It is revoked when a newer link goes to the same signer, or when the request closes. Opens are rate-limited.
    - An expired, revoked or used link shows an explanation page with the landlord's contact details.
- **The signing page** (`apps/links /l/[token]`):
    - **Contents:** the document as HTML; the PDF download with its SHA-256; the versioned consent text; a typed name or a drawn signature (signature_pad); "Send me a code"; code entry; a done page.
    - **Declining:** a lease can be declined, with a reason.
    - **How it is served:** server-rendered, with no cookies, a strict nonce-based CSP, `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` and `Cache-Control: no-store`.
    - **Budget:** under 1 MB; the drawing script loads only on the drawing step.
- **One-time codes:**
    - 6 digits, stored as an HMAC as in M1, valid for 10 minutes;
    - 5 wrong attempts lock signing for 30 minutes;
    - sends are limited per link (3 per 15 minutes), per phone and per IP;
    - codes go straight from the request to the SMS or email port, never through the outbox, so no plaintext code is stored;
    - they go only to the destination on record.
- **Evidence** (`signature`, append-only) records:
    - the method, and the typed name or drawn image (stored as a document);
    - the consent text version and its hash;
    - the server time in UTC;
    - the channel that verified the signer and the masked destination;
    - the IP address and user agent;
    - the document's SHA-256.
- **Finalisation** (`esign.finalise`, after the last required signature):
    1. Render the certificate page: signer names, roles, times with their time zone, verification method, IP and device, and the original document's SHA-256.
    2. Append it to the frozen PDF with `@cantoo/pdf-lib`.
    3. Store the final PDF write-once, and record its SHA-256 in the database. Both hashes go into the audit chain.
    4. Mark the lease signed, generate the schedule, and set the unit to reserved or occupied.
    5. Send every party a link to their copy. Parties are listed in the response.
- **Verification.** M1's `verifyFingerprint` re-hashes a stored file and compares it with the recorded hash.
- **Reminders** (`esign.signing-reminders`, every 15 minutes) go to unsigned signers after 24 hours, 3 days and 7 days. These are product defaults in company settings, not law. Each reminder carries a fresh link.

### 4.9 Messaging core (decisions 1 and 2)

- **`message`** (append-only) records:
    - the company, the recipient (tenant, contact, staff member or signer) and the channel (SMS or email);
    - the purpose, and the body with any link token redacted;
    - the provider and the provider's message id;
    - the cost, when the provider reports it.

    `message_event` records the status changes: queued, sent, delivered, failed.
- **`consent_event`** (append-only) records the subject, channel, grant or withdrawal, consent text version, who recorded it, when, and the source. In M3 the source is "recorded by staff"; replies and STOP arrive in M6.
- **`messaging.send`:**
    - For messages the business starts, such as signing links, ID-check links and renewal offers, it checks consent.
    - It sends on the first channel with consent, SMS then email, and records the result. WhatsApp and push join the order in M6.
    - With no channel available, it marks the message "not sent: no consent" and tells the sender.
- **Staff notifications** go by email to staff and need no consent: renewal, Rent Control, stamping and formal-lease reminders, and signing progress.
- **Test contacts.** Real adapters refuse to send to test contacts (as in M1), and the fake providers capture everything.

### 4.10 Renewals (TL-09)

- **Reminders.** `tenancy.renewal-reminders` runs daily at 08:00. It sends at 90, 60 and 30 days before the end date (a company setting) to the property's managers and the owner's users.
- **Expiring leases** for the next 90 days are listed on the home page and on their own page.
- **The renewal offer:**
    1. "Offer renewal" creates a draft lease copied from the current one. It points to the old lease (`renews_lease_id`) and starts the day after the old one ends.
    2. Staff set the new terms and issue it.
    3. The tenant and the landlord sign it through links; the signing link is the renewal offer.
    4. Once it is signed, the old lease ends on its end date and the renewal becomes active the next day, so there is no gap.

    A declined offer goes back to draft.

### 4.12 Advance rent check (AR-05, OQ-28)

- **Months demanded in advance** is the larger of the billing cycle's months and the initial advance.
- **Which limit applies** depends on the term:

    | Term | Rule |
    | --- | --- |
    | 1 month or less | `advance_rent.max_months.monthly_or_shorter` |
    | Over 6 months | `advance_rent.max_months.over_six_months` |
    | Between the two | No rule found (OQ-51), so no flag |

    The lease records the result with the rule version used.
- **What the company setting does:**

    | `advance_rent_policy` | Effect |
    | --- | --- |
    | `off` (default) | Recorded only; nothing is shown. |
    | `warn` | Flagged on the draft and the lease. |
    | `block` | The save is refused. |

- **No "compliant" wording.** Nothing ever calls an advance "compliant".
- **The seed's two-year advance** is recorded and shows no flag under `off`.

**Progress, phase 2: e-signing (2026-10-29)**

Step 6 (the e-signature engine) is done, with what the API, the worker, the tenant-app API, `apps/links` and the seed need. The web screens and the tenant-app screens are built by other sessions from the endpoints below.

- **Issuing and recalling** (`LeaseSigningService` in tenancy; `EsignService` in esign):
    - **Issue** (`POST /leases/{leaseId}/issue`, `lease.manage`), in one transaction: the draft goes to pending signatures (the terms freeze by trigger), the document is snapshotted, its PDF is requested (`requestPdf`), and the `signing_request` opens as `preparing` with one signer per lease party.
    - **Signers** are snapshotted with the phone and email on record now: the tenant's record, the tenant contact's (guarantor, signatory), the staff person's, or the party as typed (a witness with no record). Issuing refuses a lease with no tenant-side signer, no landlord's signer, no witness when the lease requires one (the company default), a signer with neither phone nor email, or dates that overlap a lease holding the unit.
    - **Recall** (`POST /leases/{leaseId}/recall`): esign cancels the request and revokes its links, and `returnToDraft` takes the lease back, in the same transaction. A request everyone has signed cannot be recalled (`signing_complete`); a signed lease is cancelled through `cancel-signed` instead.
- **Opening the request.** `documents.render-pdf` now emits `documents.rendered` in the transaction that records the PDF and its SHA-256 (its work moved into `DocumentsService.renderRequested`; the worker job wraps it). The esign consumer renders the same template and data to HTML, stores it write-once with its own hash, opens the request (`pending`, both hashes recorded) and queues every signer's link with `queueMessage`. A request recalled while its PDF rendered stays closed.
- **Links** (`secure_link`): made by the `sign` and `signed_copy` link resolvers at send time, by the hand-over endpoint, and for the tenant app; 256 random bits, only the SHA-256 stored; one signer and one purpose each; 30 days. A newer link to a signer revokes the last; a closing request revokes them all. The `signer` recipient resolver and both link resolvers are registered in `MessagingModule.onModuleInit` (`registerSigningRecipients`).
- **Consent by signer** (CM-09): a tenant or tenant contact needs consent recorded on the channel; staff need none; anyone else has none on record, so their message is "not sent: no consent", the issuer is told by email, and staff hand the link over (decision 2).
- **The links API** (`SigningPageService`; `POST /links/open`, `/links/document`, `/links/code`, `/links/sign`, `/links/decline`): the token is the only authorisation for the page. Every call is limited per address; opens also per link; code sends also per link and per destination.
    - **Opening** records the link's opens and the signer's first and last open, with the address and device as evidence on the first.
    - **Screens:** `sign` (the only one that accepts an action), `signed`, `declined`, `copy`, `expired`, `revoked` and `closed`, each explanation with the landlord's contact.
    - **One-time codes:** 6 digits, stored as an HMAC bound to the challenge, valid 10 minutes, sent straight from the request to the SMS or email port (never the outbox), only to the phone on record, or the email for an email-only signatory. No consent check: the signer asked for them (CM-09). Five wrong codes lock signing for 30 minutes; the counter survives new codes. Sends: 3 per link in 15 minutes, 10 per destination, 30 per address.
    - **Signing** checks the code first (a wrong one is counted and committed), stores a drawing write-once before the transaction that records it, then records the signature with its evidence: method, typed name or drawing (a document), consent version and SHA-256, server time, verifying channel and masked destination, address, device and the frozen PDF's SHA-256. The last required signature queues `esign.finalise`.
    - **Declining** needs a reason. The request closes, its links are revoked and `signing.declined` is emitted in one transaction; the issuer is told by email.
- **Finalisation** (`esign.finalise`, 5 retries): the frozen PDF is read back and checked against its fingerprint; the certificate (`signingCertificateV1`: names, roles, times with their zone, verification method, address, device and the frozen document's SHA-256) is rendered on the worker's Chromium pool and appended with `@cantoo/pdf-lib`; the signed copy is stored write-once; both hashes go to the audit chain (`signing.completed`); `signing.completed` is emitted; every signer is sent a `signed_copy` link; the issuer is told by email.

- **Decisions:**
    1. **How `apps/links` authenticates.** A shared key in the `x-links-key` header, compared in constant time (`LINKS_API_KEY`, required in production and refused if it is the development key).     2. **Migration `0009_esign_links`** (generated): `secure_link.purpose` and `message.link_purpose` accept `signed_copy`. Each token serves one purpose, and signing links die when the request closes, so the copy needed a purpose of its own. No tables or grants change.

The full print HTML carries 1.1 MB of inlined fonts and inline style attributes that the strict policy refuses. The page checks the stored hash before showing it.
    4. **Code before signature.** On the page the signer asks for the code, then submits the code, the typed name or drawing and the consent tick together. Architecture section 9 had the signature first; the evidence is the same, and the flow works with plain forms.
